# K-Beauty Expo Korea 2026: How Buyers Can Verify Exhibitors

**Quick answer:** An exhibitor booth is proof of participation in an event, not proof that the company manufactures cosmetics or holds a particular regulatory registration. Overseas buyers should collect each exhibitor's legal company name, booth number, role in the supply chain and supporting documents before treating it as a supplier candidate.

K-Beauty Expo Korea 2026 took place on **October 15–17, 2026**, at **KINTEX Exhibition Center 1, Halls 3–5**, in Goyang, South Korea. The show covers skincare and other cosmetics, hair and nail products, beauty equipment, ingredients, packaging and OEM/ODM services.

A trade show offers a useful head start: buyers can see products and talk to exhibitors in one place. It does not eliminate the need to distinguish brand owners from factories, compare samples and review regulatory requirements in the destination market.

## A booth number is the beginning of your research

Record the **exact exhibitor name and booth number** from the official directory. Ask whether the name on the booth belongs to the legal seller, manufacturing company, brand owner, trading company or agency. Request the Korean legal company name if it differs from the English trading name.

A practical exhibitor record should include:
- Company and brand names, booth ID and company website.
- Product type, desired sales market and claimed supply role.
- Whether the exhibitor itself produces the goods or contracts with another factory.
- Any document provided and the date it was checked.
- A short list of unresolved questions.

Do not assume every booth belongs to a Korean cosmetics manufacturer. Packaging suppliers, overseas firms, distributors and marketing companies can participate in the same event.

## Ask five questions before requesting a quote

1. **What entity will sign the purchase contract and issue the invoice?** This reveals who you will actually buy from.
2. **Who manufactures the formula and fills the finished product?** A brand may outsource production.
3. **Can you supply the relevant company registrations and facility documents?** Check the legal entity named on each document.
4. **What is the minimum order quantity for my formula and packaging?** Ask for separate terms for standard formula/private label versus custom development.
5. **Which destination markets have you supplied, and what documents can you provide?** The importing market's rules still apply.

## Cross-check Korean company registration carefully

Korea distinguishes between **cosmetics manufacturing businesses** and **responsible cosmetics sales businesses**. A legitimate brand owner may outsource manufacturing and hold a different regulatory registration from the factory that produces the goods. Thus, a company missing from a manufacturer-only dataset is not automatically suspicious. A matching company name also does not independently prove its current certifications, factory capacity or financial condition.

To avoid mistakes, begin with the legal company name and any lawfully published business identifier. Check address, registered industry type, document scope and the date the information was updated. Keep ambiguous matches for manual review.

## What buyers can do next

Build a shortlist with clearly marked **confirmed facts**, **exhibitor statements** and **questions still open**. Ask the most promising suppliers for samples and compare product specifications, documentation, lead times and commercial terms before placing a purchase order.

# Korean Cosmetics Manufacturer vs Brand Owner: Reading MFDS Records Correctly

**Quick answer:** The company that sells a Korean skincare brand is not necessarily the company that manufactures it. For supplier verification, check the legal entity and whether its documented role is **manufacturer**, **responsible cosmetics seller**, **brand owner**, **distributor** or **packaging provider**. A registration match is one useful signal, not a completed factory audit.

## Why two names can appear on one product

Imagine an overseas buyer meets an attractive Korean skincare brand at a beauty exhibition. The booth staff can discuss the serum, but the factory responsible for formulation or filling may be a different company. A third entity may export the goods. Each entity plays a different commercial role, and the documents needed from each can differ.

Korean cosmetics rules distinguish registered manufacturing operations from responsible cosmetics sales operations. In a February 2026 interpretation, Korea's Ministry of Food and Drug Safety described responsible sales activity as including cosmetics manufactured by a contractor and then marketed or distributed by the contracting party. This is why searching only the manufacturer's name is insufficient to characterize a whole brand.

## What each entity can tell you

| Entity | Main question for buyers | Evidence to request |
|---|---|---|
| Manufacturer | Who makes or fills the cosmetics? | Legal factory name, production location, relevant registration, documentation for claimed processes |
| Responsible cosmetics seller | Who is responsible for selling/quality management in Korea? | Legal entity, relevant registration and current business information |
| Brand owner | Who owns the mark and sets the product concept? | Contracting identity, trademark/brand authorization and manufacturer relationship |
| Distributor or exporter | Who will invoice, ship and support the order? | Contracting entity, export terms, product provenance and authorization |
| Packaging supplier | Who supplies bottle, tube, carton or label? | Specifications, materials, testing and quality documents, not automatic cosmetics-factory status |

**Important:** These roles can overlap. This classification is for sourcing due diligence and does not replace the full legal definition or destination-market compliance analysis.

## A four-step identity check

**Step 1 — Capture the business name exactly.** Preserve both Korean legal spelling and English trade name. Remove punctuation only as a search aid; do not discard the original value.

**Step 2 — Check official registration categories.** Use the appropriate MFDS or public-sector record for the role being assessed. Capture the last-updated date where available.

**Step 3 — Verify whether the matched entity is truly the same business.** Compare address, identifiers when lawfully available, and claimed line of business. Similar names alone are not enough.

**Step 4 — Request product- and market-specific evidence.** A legal registration does not establish every claimed certification or the suitability of one product for the US, EU or another destination market.

## When no record is found

“No match” is not a synonym for “fraud.” Possible explanations include a trade name, English transliteration, former legal name, a manufacturer that differs from the brand owner, outdated database ingestion, an excluded business type, or an overseas company outside the Korean cosmetics registry's intended scope.

Mark those cases **needs manual review**. Never silently label all nonmatches “unverified manufacturers”; that would make the database look worse than it is.

## A better way to assess a sourcing database

When checking an exhibitor list against a Korean cosmetics dataset, first count businesses that should reasonably appear in that particular dataset. Exclude unrelated overseas exhibitors, display agencies, packaging-only firms and other out-of-scope records. Report manufacturer matches and responsible-seller matches separately and retain an unresolved category.

This disciplined approach makes GloSource AI's sourcing evidence clearer for international buyers without promising verification that no single data source can provide.

### FAQ

**Is MFDS registration the same as ISO 22716 certification?** No. They are different types of evidence and should be assessed separately.

**Does a brand owner have to manufacture the products itself?** No. Contract manufacturing is a recognized business arrangement.

**Does a match automatically mean the supplier is safe to pay?** No. Independent commercial, quality and compliance checks remain necessary.

# How to Compare Korean Skincare OEM Samples Before Your First Order

**Quick answer:** Do not choose an OEM supplier on sample appearance or price alone. Write down a common product brief, compare all samples under the same conditions, and check identity, documentation, packaging compatibility, lead time and commercial terms. Treat sample scoring as one input into your decision—not a substitute for laboratory testing.

## Start with one shared product specification

If you ask three manufacturers for “a gentle facial serum,” they might send three completely different products. That makes a fair comparison difficult. Your brief should define the intended product type and use, target market, package format and size, formula constraints, fragrance preference, labeling needs, expected first order and desired sample deadline.

Ask each supplier whether the sample represents an **existing stock formula**, a **modified formula** or an **entirely custom development**. Those options may carry different minimums, testing plans and intellectual property terms.

## Use one scorecard for every sample

Give each dimension a score from 1 to 5, where 1 means insufficient and 5 means strongly meets the documented brief. Keep any safety, authenticity or regulatory red flags outside the numerical average—they require separate resolution.

| Dimension | Suggested weight | What to compare |
|---|---:|---|
| Product brief fit | 25% | Intended texture, appearance, use, claims and constraints |
| Documentation & traceability | 25% | Supplier identity, sample batch, specifications and requested records |
| Packaging compatibility | 15% | Container fit, closure, leakage concerns and labeling readiness |
| Communication and change control | 15% | Clear replies, tracked formulation changes and sample revision process |
| Commercial practicality | 20% | Quoted MOQ, sample cost, production timing, payment and logistics terms |

The weights are an **illustrative buyer decision aid**, not an industry certification standard. Adjust them to the product's risk and your market requirements.

## Standardize the review process

Ask for a sample ID, batch or lot reference, date received, formula/version identifier and written specification. Photograph the container and label for internal comparison. Use the same review conditions and record what changed during each revision.

For skincare, subjective feel and scent are not substitutes for stability, microbiological suitability, safety or destination-market regulatory checks. Engage a qualified testing lab and professional regulatory adviser for the product and jurisdiction as appropriate.

## Ask targeted follow-up questions

- Can the quoted MOQ change if we use standard packaging?
- Which formulation, packaging or artwork changes trigger a new sample?
- What tests or documentation are available for the proposed product?
- Who owns the formula, tooling and design outputs?
- Will the production batch match the approved reference, and how will deviations be handled?
- Who is the contracting supplier and what are the Incoterms, defect-resolution terms and lead-time commitments?

## Decide based on evidence, not promises

A polished sample from a large brand or impressive exhibition booth can make a good first impression. It does not resolve documentation gaps or prove factory capacity. Keep a separate list of **verified facts**, **supplier assertions** and **open questions** for every candidate.

### FAQ

**Should I choose the sample with the lowest quoted MOQ?** Not automatically. Compare formula scope, packaging, testing, lead time, price, quality and contractual conditions.

**Can a sample evaluation prove my product is compliant?** No. The sample checklist is a procurement tool, not a regulatory or laboratory approval.

**How many suppliers should I compare?** There is no universal number. Use as many well-qualified candidates as you need to obtain a meaningful comparison within your budget.
